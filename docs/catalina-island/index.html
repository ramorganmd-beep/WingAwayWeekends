Catalina Island

The Airport in the Sky.

Catalina’s airport sits on a ridgetop 1,602 feet above the Pacific, and landing there is a rite of passage for Southern California pilots. From the field, a shuttle winds down to Avalon, a small harbor town of golf carts, the round 1929 Casino ballroom and clear water. Stay the night, take a glass-bottom boat, ride the zip line, look for the island’s bison and watch the boats come in at sunset.

01 / Aviation & getting around

A ridgetop runway with a hump.

KAVX, Catalina, is a non-towered field at 1,602 feet with a 3,000-foot runway on a ridge. Runway 4 has a steep 2.1% downslope on final; Runway 22 (upslope) is preferred for landing. You cannot see aircraft at the other end of the runway because of the hump.

The FBO

Catalina Airport in the Sky (Catalina Island Conservancy)

Catalina Airport, Avalon, CA

562-889-0481

Open 8 a.m.–5 p.m. only; no night operations. Full-stop landings only; landing fee. Announce taking the runway on CTAF. Closed to transients on Thanksgiving and Christmas. No fuel. Over-water flight from the mainland; consider life vests and gliding distance.

Southern California coast: marine layer and low stratus most mornings from May to July (“May Gray” and “June Gloom”), usually burning off by midday; Santa Ana winds and turbulence in fall and winter; busy, complex airspace. About 1960 nm from Myrtle Beach: two long days each way with several fuel stops. Plan a week, or overnight on the way out and back.

Getting around

Shuttle to Avalon.

A shuttle or tour runs from the airport to Avalon, about 10 miles of mountain road. Avalon is walkable, with golf-cart rentals.
