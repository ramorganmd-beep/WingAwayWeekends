01 / Aviation & getting around

Land, then settle in.

KCHS is the selected arrival airport. It is a joint civil–military field shared with Joint Base Charleston; general aviation is on the east side. Compare the total FBO cost before choosing.

Alternative: Charleston Executive (KJZI) on Johns Island is quieter and closer to Kiawah, but a longer ride to the historic district.

Getting around

Uber in. Walk the peninsula.

The historic district is roughly 12 miles from the airport, typically a 20–30 minute ride depending on traffic. Confirm rideshare pickup at your FBO entrance.

Once downtown, most of this guide is walkable. Patriots Point is across the harbor in Mount Pleasant: a short ride, or the Fort Sumter ferry links it with downtown’s Liberty Square.

02 / Where to stay

Choose your kind of weekend.

Three different stays, rather than three versions of the same hotel. Rates and availability need travel dates.

Historic warehouse · Suites

The Ansonborough

Formerly the Ansonborough Inn: a converted historic warehouse at 21 Hasell Street, reopened in 2024 after a top-to-bottom renovation. Roomy suites, a short walk from the City Market, King Street and the waterfront. Call 843-723-1655 or book through its website; confirm room type and parking.

Grand mansion · Romantic splurge

Wentworth Mansion

An 1886 mansion turned inn, with period rooms, a cupola view over the city and on-site dining. It sits on the upper part of the peninsula, so plan a short ride or longer walk to the waterfront.

Official details for Wentworth Mansion
French Quarter · Steps from the water

The Vendue

An art-filled boutique hotel near Waterfront Park, with a rooftop bar that works well for a sunset drink. The most walkable of the three for the harbor, the Battery and Rainbow Row. Check rooftop hours for your dates.

Official details for The Vendue

Luxury alternatives

Grand hotel · Spa

The Charleston Place

The city’s landmark luxury hotel on Meeting Street, steps from King Street and the City Market, with the full-service spa featured below. Ideal if you want the spa on site.

Marion Square · Rooftop

Hotel Bennett

A polished luxury hotel overlooking Marion Square on upper King Street, with a rooftop bar, a spa and an elegant champagne bar. Great for a King Street shopping weekend.

Harborfront · New in 2026

The Cooper

Charleston’s first luxury waterfront hotel, opened in spring 2026 on Concord Street beside Waterfront Park and the Pineapple Fountain. Harbor views, a full-service spa, three restaurants and its own marina with sunset yacht cruises. A top-end splurge.

03 / Where to eat

Two dinners, a happy hour and brunch.

Charleston’s best tables book up, especially Friday and Saturday. Reserve your anchor dinner first.

Friday dinner · Reserve ahead

FIG

A long-running Charleston favorite for seasonal, ingredient-driven cooking, on Meeting Street. Make it your anchor reservation and keep the first afternoon flexible in case the flight runs late.

Official details for FIG
Afternoon happy hour · Oysters

Rappahannock Oyster Bar

From the Virginia family that farms Rappahannock River oysters: a lively raw bar at 701 East Bay Street, open daily from 11 AM. Perfect for oysters and a drink before the sunset sail. Check current happy-hour times; call 843-576-4693.

Official details for Rappahannock Oyster Bar
Saturday dinner · Late & intimate

Chez Nous

A tiny restaurant in a cottage at 6 Payne Court, with a handwritten menu that changes every day: two starters, two mains and two desserts, French- and Spanish-leaning. Book a later table after the sail. Only a handful of tables, so call 843-579-3060 early. Listed dinner hours run Tuesday–Sunday; confirm for your date.

Official details for Chez Nous
Sunday brunch · Southern classic

Husk

Southern cooking in a restored Victorian house on Queen Street, close to the historic core. Confirm brunch days and hours for your date before planning around it.

04 / Things to do

Walk the old city. Climb aboard a carrier.

Choose a few memorable experiences; you do not need to fit every attraction into one weekend.

01

King Street shopping

Charleston’s main shopping street, best walked in sections: antiques, galleries and fine jewelry on Lower King, boutiques and familiar names in the middle, and design shops and restaurants on Upper King. Plan an unhurried afternoon.

02

The Battery, Waterfront Park & Rainbow Row

The classic Charleston walk: the Pineapple Fountain in Waterfront Park, the pastel houses of Rainbow Row on East Bay Street, then the harbor-side promenade at the Battery. Free, flexible and best in the morning or toward sunset.

03

Patriots Point & the USS Yorktown

The pilot’s pick. Admission covers the aircraft carrier USS Yorktown with its collection of historic aircraft, the destroyer USS Laffey, the Vietnam Experience and the Medal of Honor Museum. The museum lists daily hours of 10 AM–5 PM with the ticket office closing at 4 PM; it occasionally closes for events, so check the news page before you go. Allow at least two to three hours.

Patriots Point visitor information
04

Fort Sumter

Where the Civil War began, reached only by the park’s official ferry, Fort Sumter Tours. Boats leave from Liberty Square downtown and from Patriots Point; the round trip takes about 2¼ hours, including roughly an hour at the fort. Leaving from Patriots Point pairs naturally with the Yorktown. Weather and tides can affect departures.

National Park Service visitor FAQ
05

A foodie walking tour

About two and a half hours of tastings and stories with a local guide, a great first-day introduction to the city’s food. Bulldog Tours’ Savor the Flavors (starting near the City Market) and Charleston Culinary Tours’ downtown walk are two well-reviewed options. Book ahead and check current prices and times.

06

Evening carriage ride

Clip-clop through the old walled city as the gas lamps come on. Palmetto Carriage Works and Old South Carriage Co. both run early-evening tours (listed around 5:30–8 PM), including private rides for two and a haunted-history option. Evening tours often sell out, so book ahead; carriages pause in extreme heat.

Palmetto Carriage Works

One water outing is enough

Sunset sail · Tall ship

Schooner Pride

A two-hour sail on an 84-foot, three-masted schooner from Aquarium Wharf, timed for sunset, past Fort Sumter, the Yorktown and the Ravenel Bridge. Lightly narrated; drinks available on board. Confirm the current schedule and price.

Official details for Schooner Pride

Fort Sumter Tours

If Fort Sumter is on your list, this counts as your water outing. Book the departure point that fits your day: Liberty Square or Patriots Point.

05 / Shopping & boutiques

Bring home something with a story.

King Street is the spine: antiques and galleries on Lower King, fashion and familiar names in the middle, design and dining on Upper King.

Historic shopping street

King Street

Walk it in sections rather than end to end. Lower King, below Market Street, suits antiques and fine jewelry; middle King has boutiques and well-known brands; Upper King leans toward design shops and restaurants.

Market stalls · Local crafts

Charleston City Market

One of the country’s oldest public markets, with local artisans, sweetgrass baskets and gifts. Open daily; ask about its evening night market for your dates.

Fine jewelry · Since 1919

Croghan’s Jewel Box

A family-owned King Street jeweler and a Charleston institution for estate pieces and gifts. A memorable stop on a couples weekend.

07 / Breweries & craft drinks

Make room for a local pour.

Charleston’s brewery district runs along “the Neck,” north of downtown. Pick one stop; it is a short ride, not a walk.

Brewery district · Brewpub

Edmund’s Oast

Brewery and restaurant at 1081 Morrison Drive, with a separate taproom on upper King Street. The best choice if you want food with your beer.

09 / Suggested itinerary

Friday in. Sunday home.

A sample Friday–Sunday weekend: one special dinner, one casual evening, aviation history and the harbor. No bookings or availability confirmed.

Day trip version

Just for the day

  1. Arrive

    Land at KCHS mid-morning and ride to Patriots Point.

  2. Midday

    Explore the USS Yorktown and its aircraft, then lunch downtown.

  3. Afternoon

    A walk past Rainbow Row to the Battery, then back to the FBO.

FRI

Arrive & unwind

  1. Late-morning arrival

    Land at KCHS, subject to the actual flight plan, and ride downtown. Ask your hotel to hold luggage if the room is not ready.

  2. Afternoon

    A foodie walking tour, which doubles as lunch, then an unhurried King Street shopping afternoon. Check in when your room is ready.

  3. Evening

    An early-evening carriage ride through the old city, then dinner at FIG if reserved. A nightcap at The Vendue’s rooftop if the weather is good.

SAT

Carrier deck & harbor

  1. Morning

    Ride to Patriots Point when it opens. Explore the USS Yorktown and its aircraft, then take the Fort Sumter ferry from Patriots Point if you want to add the fort.

  2. Afternoon

    Back downtown: Waterfront Park, Rainbow Row and the Battery. Optional couples massage at The Charleston Place instead, if timing allows.

  3. Evening

    Happy hour at Rappahannock Oyster Bar, the Schooner Pride sunset sail from nearby Aquarium Wharf, then a later dinner at Chez Nous.

SUN

An easy departure

  1. Morning

    Brunch at Husk, if open, and a last walk through the historic district.

  2. Before leaving

    Check out and return to the FBO with ample time for aircraft preparation.

  3. Fly home

    Let actual weather and your flight plan set the departure. Leave room to linger over a last coffee.

Longer stay · Add a day

With an extra day

  1. Morning

    A slow morning on Lower King for antiques and galleries.

  2. Afternoon

    Fort Sumter by ferry from Liberty Square, then a brewery stop at Edmund’s Oast.

  3. Evening

    Dinner at Halls Chophouse.

10 / Good to know

Keep the weekend easy to change.

These details make the difference between an appealing list and a usable getaway.

Book in this order

If a game or event drives the trip, secure tickets first. Then flexible lodging, the Friday dinner reservation and one water outing.

Allow for the season

Summers are hot and humid with afternoon storms; spring and fall are peak seasons. Coastal weather can disrupt flights, sails and the Fort Sumter ferry.

Watch your footing

Historic streets include cobblestones, uneven sidewalks and older buildings with stairs. Comfortable shoes help; confirm elevator access with lodging.

Dates come next

Hotel prices, tickets, hours and rideshare fares are date-dependent. No availability or live price is implied here.
