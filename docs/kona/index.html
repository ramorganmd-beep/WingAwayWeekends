Kona & the Big Island

Lava, mantas and Kona coffee.

The Kona coast is the dry, sunny side of the Big Island, where black lava fields run down to white-sand coves and resorts. Land at Kona International, stay on the Kohala Coast, snorkel with manta rays at night, tour a Kona coffee farm, visit the ancient place of refuge at Puuhonua o Honaunau and make the long drive (or a short flight to Hilo) to Hawaii Volcanoes National Park.

01 / Aviation & getting around

Land on the Kona coast.

PHKO (KOA), Ellison Onizuka Kona International, is a towered airport at 51 feet with an 11,000-foot runway on the lava fields north of Kailua-Kona. Hilo (PHTO) is closer to the national park.

The FBO

Atlantic Aviation Kona

Kona International Airport, Kailua-Kona, HI

808-334-0699

Other FBOs: Big Island Jet Center (808-867-2452) and Signature Aviation (808-329-4682). Long over-water legs from the other islands; carry flotation. Volcanic haze (vog) can reduce visibility; check for volcanic-activity TFRs over the national park. 100LL and Jet A listed.

Hawaiian flying: steady trade winds with strong gusts and mechanical turbulence near terrain and in the channels between islands, afternoon showers and low clouds over the mountains, sudden obscuration in valleys, and long over-water legs. Carry life vests and consider a raft. Hawaii is about 2,100 nm of open ocean from California, beyond the range of most light aircraft. These guides are for Hawaii-based pilots and for visitors who rent or fly with a local operator, typically from Honolulu (PHNL), Kalaeloa (PHJR) or Kahului (PHOG).
