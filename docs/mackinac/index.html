Mackinac Island

No cars. Just horses, bikes and the Grand Hotel’s porch.

Cars have been banned on Mackinac Island since the 1890s, so the clip-clop of horses and the ring of bicycle bells set the pace. Land on the island’s own runway, take a horse-drawn taxi to the Grand Hotel, bike the eight-mile shore road, explore an 18th-century fort and sample the famous fudge.

01 / Aviation & getting around

Land on the island itself.

KMCD, Mackinac Island Airport, sits on the island’s plateau, about a mile and a half from downtown. It is run by the Mackinac Island State Park Commission.

The FBO

Mackinac Island Airport (state park; not a full-service FBO)

Mackinac Island, MI 49757

906-847-3231FBO details

Call ahead about landing and parking fees, fuel, hours and seasonal operations. The island is busiest from June to August; arrange a horse-drawn taxi to town from the airport.

Runway 8/26 is about 3,500 feet, on high ground with trees at both ends; Lake Huron winds bring crosswinds and fog. Brief performance and an alternate such as Pellston. About 780 nm from Myrtle Beach: plan a fuel stop, and consider a third night.

Getting around

Leave the car behind (you have to).

Walk, rent bikes or take horse-drawn taxis and carriage tours. The island is about eight miles around.

04 / Things to do

Forts, bluffs and the shore road.

Everything is by bike, horse or on foot.

01

Fort Mackinac

A hilltop fort built by the British in 1780, with cannon and rifle firings and views over the harbor.

Tickets
02

Bike around the island

M-185, the only state highway with no cars, runs eight flat miles around the shore.

03

Arch Rock

A natural limestone arch 146 feet above the water, in Mackinac Island State Park.

04

A carriage tour

A narrated horse-drawn tour of the island’s history, bluffs and cottages.

05

The Grand Hotel porch

Have a drink or afternoon tea on the 660-foot porch overlooking the Straits.
