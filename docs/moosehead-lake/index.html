Moosehead Lake & Greenville

A seaplane town on Maine’s biggest lake.

Greenville sits at the south end of Moosehead Lake, 40 miles of wild water ringed by mountains, and seaplanes are part of daily life. Land on the lake or at the town airport, stay at the Blair Hill Inn high above the water, cruise on the 1914 steamboat Katahdin, look for moose at dusk and climb Mount Kineo’s cliffs. In September, the International Seaplane Fly-In fills the cove with floatplanes.

01 / Aviation & getting around

Land on the lake or at the town airport.

3B1, Greenville Municipal, is a non-towered field with a 4,000-foot runway just outside town. Floatplanes and amphibians can use 52B, Moosehead Aero Marine, right in town.

The FBO

Greenville Municipal Airport (Town of Greenville)

Greenville Municipal Airport, Greenville, ME

207-717-5155

Confirm fuel, fees and a ride before you go. No live fuel price is shown.

Maine mountain and lake weather: fog over the lakes in the morning, fast-changing summer storms, gusty winds funneling down the lakes, and ice from about mid-November to mid-May. About 830 nm from Myrtle Beach: plan a fuel stop and three nights.

Seaplane access

Moosehead Aero Marine (52B), public-use seaplane base

Greenville, ME · manager 207-299-4522

207-299-4522

Water lanes: 14W/32W 6,000 × 1,200 ft; 18W/36W 5,000 × 1,000 ft.

Currier’s seaplane base (21M) is 1.1 miles west on the same CTAF, 122.9; monitor Greenville Municipal’s CTAF, 122.8, for local traffic. (FAA chart supplement remarks.)

Bring your own ropes to secure the aircraft at the dock. Expect recreational boats in summer. The lake is usually iced over from mid-November to mid-May.

Water landing rules, conditions and docking change; call the base and check the chart supplement before you go.

Getting around

Car or shuttle.

Downtown Greenville is walkable from the docks; a car helps for Lily Bay and the Kineo boat.
