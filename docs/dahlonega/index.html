Dahlonega

Gold-rush town. Mountain wineries.

America’s first major gold rush began here in 1828. Today Dahlonega’s historic square is lined with tasting rooms, shops and restaurants, and the surrounding mountains hold some of Georgia’s best wineries. Add a waterfall hike and it is an easy, romantic weekend.

01 / Aviation & getting around

Land by the lake, drive into the hills.

KGVL, Lee Gilmer Memorial in Gainesville, is about 30–35 minutes from Dahlonega by car, near Lake Lanier.

The FBO

Champion Aviation Services

Lee Gilmer Memorial · Phillips 66

770-532-4136

Recent pilot reports say there is no courtesy car, so arrange a rental car before you go and confirm pickup at the airport. A second operator on the field, Lanier Flight Center, also sells fuel. Ask about two-night parking and fees. No live fuel price is shown.

The North Georgia mountains rise to the north. Review terrain, the sectional and current NOTAMs, and expect afternoon storms in summer.

Getting around

Walk the square; ride to the wineries.

The square is walkable, with several tasting rooms on it. The vineyards are 10–20 minutes out on winding mountain roads, so book a wine-tour driver for the tasting day.

Pilot note / Wine & flying

Taste on the non-flying day.

FAA rules (14 CFR 91.17) require at least 8 hours from bottle to throttle, a blood alcohol below 0.04% and no lingering effects. Many pilots use 12–24 hours as a personal minimum, since a hangover can impair you long after the legal limit is met. Plan the tasting day for the middle of the weekend, keep the evening before departure dry, and let a driver or shuttle handle the roads. If the pilot wants a full tasting day, add a third night. Many wineries take reservations on weekends; check before you go.

05 / Things to do

Gold, waterfalls and mountain air.

Balance the tasting day with something outdoors.

01

The historic square

Boutiques, galleries, candy shops and tasting rooms around the 1836 courthouse. Lovely in the evening.

02

Dahlonega Gold Museum

Inside the old courthouse on the square, telling the story of the 1828 gold rush and the federal mint that once stood in town.

03

A gold-mine tour

Go underground at a historic mine outside town, and try panning for gold. Fun and a little corny.

04

Amicalola Falls State Park

One of the tallest waterfalls in the Southeast, about 30 minutes west, with a staircase trail and a lodge. A great Sunday-morning walk.

Optional / Spa & relaxation
