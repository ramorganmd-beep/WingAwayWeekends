Will the weather work for my weekend?

Check both ends of your trip a few hours either side of when you plan to leave: flight category, your own minimums, and the crosswind on the best runway against your limit.

For planning only. This outlook helps you choose a weekend and a departure time. It is not a weather briefing. Before every flight, confirm the weather and your route with your flight planning resources, such as ForeFlight, Garmin Pilot or 1800wxbrief.com, and get an official briefing. The pilot in command makes the go or no-go decision.

Times are local at each airport · Forecast run:

A planning outlook, not a weather briefing. Forecasts come from NOAA’s National Blend of Models station guidance: ceiling and visibility every 3 hours out to 3 days, then wind, gusts, sky cover, rain and thunderstorm chances every 12 hours out to about 8 days. Long-range forecasts change from run to run, so each leg shows how the forecast for your planned time has changed since the runs 12 and 24 hours earlier. Leave and Come home times are local at the departure airport; landing times are local at the arrival airport. Take-offs and landings after sunset, after dark (end of civil twilight) or before sunrise are noted; choose “Daylight only” to count them against a time. Crosswinds use the gust speed on the runway with the least crosswind (FAA runway data; true headings). Flight categories: VFR above 3,000 ft and 5 mi; MVFR 1,000–3,000 ft or 3–5 mi; IFR 500–999 ft or 1–3 mi; LIFR below that. Advisories near your route (G-AIRMETs, SIGMETs, Center Weather Advisories and pilot reports of icing or turbulence, from the Aviation Weather Center) appear from about 12 hours before departure, checked within 25 nm of the straight-line route; your actual route, altitude and timing may differ. Always confirm with your flight planning resources and get an official briefing (ForeFlight, Garmin Pilot or 1800wxbrief.com) before you fly; the pilot in command makes the go or no-go decision.
