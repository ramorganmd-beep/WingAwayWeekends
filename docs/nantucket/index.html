Nantucket

Fly out to the island. Ride a bike to the lighthouse.

Thirty miles off Cape Cod, Nantucket was once the whaling capital of the world. Today its cobblestone town, gray-shingled cottages and wide beaches make it one of New England’s great escapes. Fly straight to the island, bike to Sconset, watch the sunset at Madaket and dine by the harbor.

01 / Aviation & getting around

Land on the island.

KACK, Nantucket Memorial, is a towered airport about three miles from town, with a town-run FBO next to the terminal.

The FBO

Nantucket Memorial Airport FBO

14 Airport Road, Nantucket, MA 02554

508-325-5307

Summer is extremely busy; ask the FBO about parking availability and reservations well ahead, and confirm hours and fees. No live fuel price is shown.

It is about 25 nm over open water from Cape Cod, and fog can close the island for hours, especially in late spring and early summer. Carry appropriate safety gear, plan a mainland alternate such as Hyannis, and expect a fuel stop on the way.

Getting around

Leave the car behind.

Taxis and seasonal shuttle buses run from the airport. On the island, bikes are the best way around; paved paths run to Sconset, Surfside and Madaket.

04 / Things to do

Cobblestones, lighthouses and beaches.

Slow down. This is an island for bikes, beaches and long lunches.

01

The Whaling Museum

The Nantucket Historical Association’s museum in a former candle factory, with a 46-foot sperm whale skeleton and the island’s whaling story.

Museum information
02

Bike to Sconset

A flat, paved 8-mile path to the village of Siasconset, with rose-covered cottages, the Bluff Walk and Sankaty Head Lighthouse.

03

Sunset at Madaket

The island’s classic sunset beach, at the end of the western bike path.

04

Brant Point Lighthouse

The little lighthouse at the harbor entrance. Tradition says toss a penny as the ferry passes to promise a return.

05

Great Point

Over-sand tours run out to the lighthouse and seal colonies at the island’s wild northern tip.
