Gettysburg

Hallowed ground, colonial taverns and apple country.

In July 1863, the largest battle of the Civil War was fought in the fields around this small Pennsylvania town, and Lincoln gave his Gettysburg Address here that November. Tour the battlefield with a licensed guide in your own car, visit Eisenhower’s farm, and dine by candlelight in a 1776 tavern.

01 / Aviation & getting around

Land three miles from the battlefield.

KW05, Gettysburg Regional Airport, is a small field just west of town, a few minutes from the battlefield and downtown.

The FBO

Blouch Aircraft Services

1150 Chambersburg Road, Gettysburg, PA 17325

717-357-5958

Directories list a courtesy car and rental cars; taxis and rideshares are scarce, so arrange a car ahead. Confirm the address, hours, parking and fees with the FBO. No live fuel price is shown.

Camp David’s prohibited area P-40 lies about 15 nm southwest, and the Washington, DC SFRA is farther south. Brief both, check TFRs before every flight, and review the sectional and current NOTAMs.

Getting around

A car makes it easy.

Battlefield tours are done in your own car with a guide, and downtown is walkable once you park.

04 / Things to do

Hallowed ground.

Start at the Museum and Visitor Center, then see the battlefield with a guide.

01

Museum and Visitor Center

The film, the museum and the restored Gettysburg Cyclorama, a 377-foot painting of Pickett’s Charge. Allow two to three hours.

Park information
02

A licensed battlefield guide

A two-hour private tour in your car with a Licensed Battlefield Guide, the best way to understand the three days. Book ahead through the Gettysburg Foundation.

03

Little Round Top & the High Water Mark

Two of the battle’s most famous places, with sweeping views across the fields.

04

Eisenhower National Historic Site

Ike’s farm and weekend retreat, reached by shuttle from the Visitor Center.

Shuttle and tours
05

Adams County orchards and wineries

Apple country and small wineries just outside town. Taste on the non-flying day.
