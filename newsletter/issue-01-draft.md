## This month’s getaway: Tucson

November through April is desert flying season, and Tucson is the place to start. Land at Tucson International, spend a morning at the **Pima Air & Space Museum** among more than 400 aircraft (book the bus tour of the Davis-Monthan boneyard ahead), drive through **Saguaro National Park** at sunset, and stay at the 1930 **Arizona Inn**, a quiet compound of pink adobe casitas and rose gardens. Save room for Sonoran food. Tucson is a UNESCO City of Gastronomy.

**Pilot note:** KTUS is Class C, with military traffic from Davis-Monthan next door. Brief the procedures, and plan morning departures before the afternoon bumps.

## Fly to Lunch: Joe’s One-Niner Diner, KPRB

A reader sent this one in, and it’s a gem. Joe’s One-Niner Diner sits inside the Paso Robles terminal, named for the airport’s runway, with big windows over the ramp. Breakfast and lunch, reportedly 6 a.m. to 2 p.m. (7 a.m. on Sundays), with a cash discount. The Estrella Warbirds Museum is on the same field, and you’re 15 minutes from some of California’s best wine country.

[See it on Fly to Lunch →](https://wingawayweekends.com/fly-to-lunch/)

## From the mailbag: Prescott, the way locals fly it

Another reader sent three favorites around Prescott Regional (KPRC), and all three are now in the guide:

- **Susie’s Skyway Restaurant**, breakfast and lunch in the terminal with runway views
- **Manzanita Grill** at Antelope Hills Golf Courses, right next to the field
- **Rugged Pilot Coffee**, a pilot-themed roaster and gear shop near the airport

Pilots report parking at Cutter Aviation and getting a ride over, so call ahead.
